# Playbook — "How's my team looking?"

Rohit has asked an ad-hoc question about the squad, off-cycle. Give him a straight, phone-readable read on where the team stands right now. This is a summary, not a decision — no transfer executes off this, and it needs no `yes`.

## Ground yourself first

- Read the **season snapshot** (`season-state.json`): current squad, bank, free transfers, chips, captain/vice, current gameweek.
- Read the **projections** if available (`projections.csv` / latest report): projected points per player and for the XI.
- Everything you say comes from these. **Do not invent** a stat, a fixture, an injury, or a number that isn't in front of you. If you want to reference news, it must be from the Scout log or a cited source — otherwise leave it out.

## What to say

1. **One-line state** — squad value, bank, FT banked, chips left, captain. Orient him fast.
2. **Concrete strengths** — name players and why, grounded in the snapshot and projections. "Haaland + Bruno anchor the premiums; both projecting well." Cite the projected points where they carry the point.
3. **Concrete risks** — real ones from the data: thin bench, a flagged player, a tough run, an underperformer. Name names.
4. **Bottom line** — a one-line read on the team's overall shape.

## Rules

- Separate facts (from the snapshot/projections, with the number) from your judgments (declared as reads). Never blur them.
- **Flag stale data.** If the snapshot's last update or the projections look old (e.g. last check is days back, or gameweek doesn't match), say so up front — a confident read on stale data is worse than a caveat.
- Keep it tight and scannable on a phone. Headline first, short lines, no essay. This is a status read, not a briefing.
